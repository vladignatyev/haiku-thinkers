# Six Hats Thinking Framework

Edward de Bono's six hats method is a powerful technique for structured parallel thinking. Each hat represents a distinct type of thinking mode.

## The Six Hats

### White Hat (Facts & Information)
- **Focus**: Facts, figures, information needs
- **Questions asked**:
  - What do we know for certain?
  - What information is missing?
  - How can we get the information we need?
  - What questions haven't been answered?
- **Output**: Clear separation of known facts from assumptions

### Red Hat (Emotions & Intuition)
- **Focus**: Feelings, hunches, creative instincts, aesthetic judgments
- **Questions asked**:
  - What does my gut tell me?
  - What's my emotional reaction?
  - What are others likely feeling?
  - What would a creative or intuitive approach look like?
- **Output**: Emotional landscape and intuitive insights

### Black Hat (Critical Thinking & Risks)
- **Focus**: Judgment, risks, weaknesses, logical flaws, contradictions
- **Questions asked**:
  - What could go wrong?
  - What are the weaknesses?
  - What's logically inconsistent?
  - What's the worst-case scenario?
  - Why might this fail?
- **Output**: Risk map, contradiction analysis, failure modes

### Yellow Hat (Optimism & Benefits)
- **Focus**: Positive potential, benefits, strengths, opportunities
- **Questions asked**:
  - What are the best-case scenarios?
  - What are the benefits?
  - What's the best version of this?
  - What opportunities does this create?
  - Why might this succeed?
- **Output**: Opportunity analysis, value proposition, success scenarios

### Green Hat (Creativity & Alternatives)
- **Focus**: Lateral thinking, new ideas, alternatives, unconventional approaches
- **Questions asked**:
  - What are other ways to approach this?
  - What would a completely different solution look like?
  - What if we removed this constraint?
  - What haven't we considered?
  - What's a creative alternative?
- **Output**: Alternative approaches, innovative solutions, new directions

### Blue Hat (Process & Synthesis)
- **Focus**: Meta-thinking, process control, overview, conclusions
- **Questions asked**:
  - What have we learned from all these perspectives?
  - What's the core insight?
  - What should we do next?
  - Are we missing anything?
  - What's the integrated conclusion?
- **Output**: Synthesis, decision, action plan, next steps

## Why This Works

**Parallel thinking, not sequential**: In normal discussion, people wear different hats simultaneously, leading to confusion and debate. Sequential hat-wearing—everyone thinking from the same perspective at the same time—eliminates interference.

**Legitimizes different thinking modes**: It's okay to be emotional (Red Hat) without being criticized for not being logical. It's okay to be critical (Black Hat) without dismissing opportunities. Each mode has its place.

**Removes ego from thinking**: By assigning thinking modes to hats instead of people, criticism becomes depersonalized. You're not wrong; you're just wearing the wrong hat for that moment.

**Comprehensive coverage**: The six hats ensure nothing gets missed—facts, feelings, risks, opportunities, creativity, and synthesis all get examined.

## Multi-Agent Application

When six agents each wear a different hat:

1. They work in parallel (faster analysis)
2. Each brings their full reasoning to one perspective
3. The Blue Hat agent synthesizes without politics or compromise
4. You get a genuinely comprehensive view of the problem

The key is that each agent analyzes independently first, then the Blue Hat synthesizes—not debating, but integrating.
